# Universal Credit
Universal Credit can top up low or irregular earnings for people on a low income, including people in work, part-time workers and some students. Eligibility depends on income, savings, age, and living situation. You can check using the free benefits calculators on Citizens Advice, Turn2us or gov.uk. Checking does not commit you to anything.

# Help to Save
Help to Save is a government scheme that gives a 50% bonus on savings for people receiving Universal Credit or Working Tax Credit. You can save up to £50 a month and get a bonus of up to £1,200 over four years. Check current rules on gov.uk.

# Help with energy and bills
Ask your energy supplier about hardship funds, payment plans and the Warm Home Discount. Ask your mobile or broadband provider about social tariffs, which are cheaper plans for people on low incomes. Your council may offer a local welfare scheme for emergencies.

# Free help
Citizens Advice (free, confidential advice on money, housing and benefits), MoneyHelper (free government-backed money guidance), StepChange and National Debtline (free debt advice). Many offer phone, webchat, and interpreter support in other languages.

# Savings protection
Money in UK-authorised banks and building societies is protected by the FSCS up to £120,000 per person per firm. Easy-access savings accounts let you take money out any time. Check you are using an authorised provider.
