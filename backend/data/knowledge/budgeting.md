# Budgeting with irregular income
Start from your lowest normal week of pay, not your best week. Cover rent, energy, food, travel to work and debt minimums first. Anything above that is a bonus you can split between a buffer and spending. Check money once a week for five minutes rather than daily; a short regular check is less stressful and catches problems early.

# Emergency buffer
A first target of £500 is realistic and stops most small shocks (a broken phone, a missed shift) turning into overdraft or loans. Move a small fixed amount on payday, even £5 to £10. Keep it in a separate pot so it is out of sight. After £500, build towards one month of essential costs, then three months.

# Overdrafts
An arranged overdraft is borrowing and usually costs interest. Check what your bank charges (the rate is shown as EAR). Try to be back in credit on payday, then keep a small positive cushion. If you are regularly using the overdraft, it is a sign the monthly budget does not fit, not a personal failing. Talk to your bank; ask about hardship support or a lower overdraft limit that stops the habit.

# Buy now pay later
BNPL such as Klarna is often interest-free but missed payments can lead to fees and can affect your credit file. Pay off the plan with the smallest balance first and avoid starting a new one until the current plan ends. Do not borrow to pay for BNPL.

# Subscriptions
List every subscription once a quarter. Cancel anything unused. A gym or streaming service you do not use is money that could build your buffer. Many can be paused rather than cancelled.

# Priority debts
If you cannot pay everything, prioritise things with serious consequences: rent, energy, council tax, court fines. Free, impartial debt advice is available from StepChange, Citizens Advice and National Debtline. Never pay for a debt advice service.
